# Log stream scale spike (issue 7515): results

Raw data is in `results/`; `python3 analyze.py results` regenerates every table below. The sweep is `run.sh`.
This write-up was cut short on request: the tables are complete except where noted under "Not measured".

## Method

- One process per run, one engine per process: the in-process substrate harness (`aether-harness-substrate`), 8 pool workers, release build, on a 32-thread x86-64 Linux host with about 60 GB of memory. The host was shared: other compile jobs ran during parts of the sweep (`results/host-load.txt` has the load average at each stage start; values above about 8 are someone else's work). Cores were not pinned.
- Every cell is `median [min..max]` over 5 fresh-process reps (10 for the closed-tap rows) unless the reps column says otherwise.
- A *logging handler* is one dispatched mail whose handler logs one 80-byte line. Unless marked `(tracing)`, the handler pushes into its ring exactly as the host does for a guest log call (`emit_host_event`); the `(tracing)` rows go through the native `tracing` stack, stderr discarded.
- Paced rows offer the load from a `Tick` subscriber under the tick's chain at 60 frames per second, so a frame's time is the time until its handlers, the gatherer's `Tick`, and the subscriber's batch have all run. Flood rows run frames back to back. Overload rows run producers that re-mail themselves on their own chains as fast as the workers dispatch them, while frames are still offered at 60 per second.
- The gatherer is a real native actor with a real subscriber actor. It sends one batch per `Tick`, sorted by time, each line named by actor path. The subscriber decodes the batch and keeps a 10,000-line scrollback.
- CPU is process user+system time (10 ms ticks) over the measured frames.

Modes:

| mode | what it is |
|---|---|
| `nohook` | the gather compiled out of `dispatch_envelope`: main's dispatch path |
| `off` | the gather compiled in, tap closed (no subscriber) |
| `mail` | issue design A with the slice mailed with **no lineage** (not a settlement root); pending cap 4096, one batch of up to 4096 per tick |
| `mailroot` | issue design A as worded: each slice on a **fresh causal chain** |
| `buffer` | the alternative: one bounded buffer on the `Mailer` (4096 lines, drop-oldest, one lock), drained per tick, batch cap 256 |
| `shard` | the same buffer split over 16 locks picked by worker thread (4096 lines total) |
| `mail256`, `buffer4k` | each design with the other's batch cap |

## Findings

1. **Tap closed costs nothing measurable.** `off` against `nohook`: -1.2 to -2.6 ns wall per dispatched mail (the sign is noise; the middle half of reps spans 5 to 30 ns). The repository's own comparison (`results/stock-*.md`, 12 interleaved trials against unmodified main): 0 regressed, 93 stable, 51 withheld as bistable on latency; 0 of 10 throughput cells moved.
2. **Tap open, handler logs nothing:** about +5 ns wall, +26 ns CPU per dispatched mail, all designs.
3. **At 100 logging handlers per frame every design is small:** +6 to +182 us per frame (0.0 to 1.1% of 16.6 ms).
4. **At 10,000 logging handlers per frame the mail design alone breaks the frame budget:** frame p50 18.5 to 21.5 ms against 2.8 to 5.3 ms off (+13 to +18 ms). Single buffer +3.2 to +5.2 ms; sharded buffer +0.9 to +2.1 ms. Added CPU per logging handler: mail about 5,000 ns, single buffer 2,000 to 3,000 ns, sharded 600 to 860 ns.
5. **Under sustained overload the mail design is unbounded.** The gatherer's inbox is an unbounded channel. With only 4 producers looping it reached 16.8 million queued slices and 8.3 GB resident in about 30 s (the run's 8 GB guard ended most reps), 13 frames completed in the flood, frame p50 709 ms, worst frame 12.5 s. The frame waits because the gatherer's `Tick` queues behind the slices. With 1000 producers looping it hit the guard in 9.8 s with 2 frames completed. Both chassis drivers abort a frame that does not settle in 30 s (`FRAME_SETTLEMENT_CAP`); the guard stopped the flood before that.
6. **The rooted mail (the issue's "fresh chain") kills the engine.** Each slice is a settlement root, and the settlement table is a fixed 16,384 slots that panics when full (`chassis/settlement_table.rs`, "settlement table saturated"). Workers panic one by one and the engine stops. Seen at 10,000 logging handlers per frame (paced: 2 of 5 reps died; flood: every rep) and under overload.
7. **Both buffers stay bounded and recover at once.** Resident memory flat (54 MiB against 42 off with 4 loopers), backlog zero within one frame of the flood ending. The single lock is itself a choke under saturation: with every worker logging, production fell from 4.56 to 1.65 million lines per second and frame p50 rose from 81 to 227 ms. The sharded buffer held 4.18 million and 106 ms.
8. **Accounting adds up.** Unaccounted is 0 in every paced and flood configuration and in the 4-looper overload. In the 1000-looper overload the subscriber was told of 4 (mail) and 23 (buffer) more lines than the producers logged, summed over 4 reps of about 50 to 70 million lines each: over-reported, not lost; not traced further (lines from engine actors are gathered too and are the likely source).
9. **The batch is the subscriber's cost.** A 4096-line batch costs the gatherer 1.7 to 2.7 ms to build and the subscriber about 3 ms to decode and store, serial on the frame's chain: about a third of a frame. A 256-line batch costs 0.2 to 0.7 ms and 0.2 ms. At 10,000 lines per frame a 256 cap delivers 2.6% of lines and reports the rest skipped; a 4096 cap delivers 41%.
10. **Backfill is a one-time burst of 50 to 140 ms.** 1000 actors with full rings: mail 70 ms then 136 ms frames and +117 MiB; single buffer one 81 ms frame; sharded one 48 ms frame. 1,024,000 lines move and 4096 (mail) or 256 (buffer) are delivered. Without backfill: no frame over 2.3 ms, nothing moved.

## Not measured

- **Real wasm guest loggers.** Not built. The default log path in these rows is the host half of a guest log call; the guest half (the wasm call and the copy out of guest memory) is the same in every mode. A wasm component's host actor (`WasmTrampoline`) is dispatched by the same `dispatch_envelope`.
- **A wasm subscriber.** The console here is native; a guest console's decode cost is not measured.
- **The flush after `unwire`** (a closing actor's last lines) is not implemented in the spike.
- **No level filter** on the tap; every line at or above the ring's own filter is gathered.
- Overload with 1000 loopers has 4 reps, not 5 (stopped early), and `mailroot` never finished a rep there. `mail256`, `buffer4k`, 10-lines and `(tracing)` rows ran while another job loaded the host; read their spreads.
- The closed-tap difference is below what this host can resolve; the claim is "under a few nanoseconds", not a number.
